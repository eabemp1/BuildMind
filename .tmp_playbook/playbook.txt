2. Onboarding — The Problem and the Fix
Adarsh getting confused and calling BuildMind a productivity app was not a positioning failure. It was an onboarding failure. He never experienced the product before he was asked to understand it. Reverse the sequence.
The principle: the first 60 seconds of BuildMind should feel like talking to someone who did their homework before you arrived. Not a tour. Not a setup wizard. A partner who already knows your market and has a task waiting for you.

2.1 The Onboarding Sequence — Screen by Screen
Screen 1 — The Only Question That Matters
No name field. No welcome copy. No tutorial. Just this, large, centered, in your brand voice:
"Describe your startup in one sentence."  That is the entire first screen. A single bold input. Nothing else. This takes 10 seconds, creates immediate ownership, and feeds the AI exactly what it needs.

Screen 2 — The Reflexion Strike (15 seconds after they hit enter)
The AI — powered by your 3-agent Reflexion loop — responds with something like:
"The crowded part of this market is [X]. The gap nobody has claimed yet is [Y]. Your first task: in the next 30 minutes, find one person who has this problem and send them a message. That is it."
No lock. No punishment for failure. No theatrical urgency. Just an insight that feels genuinely intelligent — because the Reflexion loop actually is. This single interaction proves what BuildMind is faster than any explanation ever could.

Screen 3 — Identity and Momentum Begin
"You are now operating like a high-execution founder. Your Momentum Score starts here."  The Momentum Score appears. The daily loop begins. The founder is already inside the product. Only now — after value has been felt — do you offer the cognitive load check-in: "Before we set your next task: how is your brain today? Fresh / Drained / Auto-pilot"

3. The Agentic Architecture — How BuildMind Becomes a Partner
A tool waits. A tool answers when asked. An agent acts — it monitors context, detects patterns, and surfaces the next move before the founder asks.
Current model (tool): User action → API call → Response
Agentic model (partner): Background process → Context update → Trigger met → Agent acts

The background process is the entire engineering challenge. BuildMind needs to do things when the founder is NOT looking at the screen.

3.1 The Founder Context Object — The Brain Behind Everything
The single most important data structure you will build. Every interaction a founder has with BuildMind gets written into a structured context object in Supabase. Not logs — a living, queryable profile. The difference between a generic AI response and one that feels like it knows the founder is entirely this object.
// Founder Context Object — stored as JSONB in Supabase
{
  founder_id: "uuid",
  startup_summary: "B2B SaaS for African SME compliance",
  current_stage: "validation",
  momentum_score: 74,
  last_active: "2026-04-25",
  days_inactive: 0,
  tasks_accepted_this_week: 3,
  tasks_overridden_this_week: 2,
  override_reasons: ["not ready", "need to think more"],
  topics_mentioned_repeatedly: ["landing page", "pricing"],
  competitor_context: { last_scan: "2026-04-24", signals: [...] },
  cognitive_pattern: "drained Mondays, fresh Thursdays",
  avoidance_signals: ["customer discovery", "cold outreach"],
  breakthrough_moments: ["validated problem with 3 users on April 12"],
  emotional_state_log: ["overwhelmed Mon", "energised Thu"],
  recovery_mode_active: false,          // NEW IN V4

BUILD THIS FIRST
BUILD THIS FIRST: This object gets updated after every interaction: every task accepted, every override, every check-in answer, every startup description update. Everything else in the agentic model depends on it.

4.4 Agent Persona Rotation — Fixing Llama Drift
NEW IN V4  
Prompt-level weekly rotation for Agent B. Implemented in lib/reflexion.ts

Agent B (The Critic) rotates its persona weekly rather than always being 'skeptical YC partner.' This prevents the AI from giving the same flavour of advice every week and keeps output feeling fresh.

Week
Agent B Persona

Week 5+
Cycle repeats. Rotation state logged in founder_context.persona_week.

4.5 Confidence Gate — The 'I Don't Know' Feature
NEW IN V4  
Builds more trust than a wrong answer. Implemented in lib/reflexion.ts

When the Reflexion loop's confidence on a recommendation is low (thin context, early stage, unusual market), the AI says it directly. Triggers when the context object has fewer than 5 data points for the relevant domain.

"I don't have enough context on your local market to give a firm answer here. Should we research competitors together, or do you want to talk to a human mentor?"
This turns BuildMind from a know-it-all into a truth-seeking partner. Honesty at the right moment builds more trust than a confident wrong answer.

Generic AI responses — the moment the AI gives advice that could apply to any founder, they stop trusting it. Every response must reference something specific from their Founder Context Object.
Streak mechanics — streaks punish absence instead of rewarding presence. Momentum Score is the replacement. Never show a broken streak counter.
Weekly email reports without personalisation — a report saying 'you completed 3 of 7 tasks' is noise. A report naming their avoidance pattern is signal.
Upgrade friction at the wrong moment — do not prompt to upgrade when the founder is frustrated. Prompt when they are winning.

9.4 Churn Prevention — The Two Danger Windows
Churn Window
Signal and Intervention
Day 3–7 (before habit forms)
No morning briefing opened. No task completed. Intervention: one personal-feeling push notification that references their startup name specifically.
Day 21–30 (novelty wears off)

Post-upgrade churn (Day 60+)
Builder user who has not used Operator features. Intervention: morning briefing surfaces one Operator-tier insight they cannot act on yet.

THE CHURN INTERVENTION RULE
THE CHURN INTERVENTION RULE: Never ask a founder if they are okay. Never say "we miss you." Instead: name the pattern. Show the cost. Offer the next step.

Example: "You have overridden 4 of the last 5 daily actions. That pattern usually means one of two things: the tasks are too hard for your current energy, or you have decided something without telling the system. Which is it?"
